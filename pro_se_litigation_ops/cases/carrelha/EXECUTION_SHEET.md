## Workstream 3 — Doc 39 (discovery stay) · Rule 72(a) window closes 2026-09-28
**State: `DO NOT FILE`**

Doc 39 is a narrow, ordinary case-management stay: Defendants answer within 14 days of
the District Judge's ruling on the motion to dismiss under Rule 12(a)(4)(A); discovery,
the Rule 26(f) report, and initial disclosures are stayed until 30 days after that answer.

This is the standard disposition in this district when a dispositive motion is pending.
Under Rule 72(a) it is reviewed for **clear error or contrary to law** — a standard this
order plainly survives. An objection would lose and would spend credibility that is
better kept for Doc 37.

The changed-circumstances theory does not rescue it either: the December 1 sale is a
reason to seek *injunctive* relief, not a reason to unstay *discovery*. Do not conflate
the two.

**State: `NO VEHICLE UNDER THE OPERATIVE PLEADING — user decision required`**

Two paths are now closed, both definitively:

**Motion to enforce Doc 34 — closed.** Doc 41 notices the sale for **December 1, 2026**,
which is **77 days** after the 9/15 notice. The 60-day condition is satisfied. Notice went
to the Court by ECF and to Plaintiffs by certified and regular mail on 9/15. Defendants
complied.

**Renewed preliminary injunction — closed as the pleading stands.** Doc 34 denied the PI
as moot because no sale was scheduled, and a scheduled sale would ordinarily revive it.
But Doc 36 states, expressly:

> "Plaintiffs do not seek in this pleading an independent quiet-title,
> wrongful-foreclosure, assignment-invalidity, securitization-invalidity, or
> loan-modification-contract cause of action."

The four surviving counts are money-damages claims: RESPA, FCRA, breach of contract, and
FDCPA. The only foreclosure-related relief requested is "the continued benefit of the
Court's existing sixty-day advance-notice requirement." A preliminary injunction requires
likelihood of success on a claim that supports that relief. **Those claims were
voluntarily abandoned**, and Judge Bagley separately found an adequate remedy at law.

**This is the cost of the amendment, and it should be named plainly.** Doc 36 is a good
pleading — it does exactly what Doc 35 demanded and is far more defensible than the
shotgun original. The trade was narrowness for survivability. But it means that as the
case is currently pleaded, **nothing on file stops the December 1 sale.**
