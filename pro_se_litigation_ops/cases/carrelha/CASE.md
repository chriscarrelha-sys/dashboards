## Orders in force
| Doc | Date | Holding | Continuing effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 34 | 2026-07-20 | Remand **denied**; prior-exclusive-jurisdiction and unanimity arguments rejected; PI **denied as moot** | **60-day notice condition** + Standing Order 18-01 referral |

**The 60-day condition, verbatim [Doc 34 at 26]:**
> "should Defendants elect to proceed with foreclosure, Defendants shall provide
> Plaintiffs (and the Court) with a minimum of 60 days advance notice."

Notice is owed to **Plaintiffs and the Court**. Where the two dates differ, the later
one starts the 60 days.

**Standing Order 18-01 referral [Doc 34 at 26]:** the motions to dismiss "remain with the
Magistrate Judge for report and recommendation along with any other pretrial proceedings."
This fixes the review standards and they must not be blended:
- **Motions to dismiss → R&R → Rule 72(b), de novo** objections within 14 days.
- **Discovery stay → nondispositive order → Rule 72(a), "clearly erroneous or contrary
  to law."**

## Why the PI was denied — and why that may have just changed
Doc 34 denied the preliminary injunction **as moot**, not on the merits:
> "With no foreclosure sale currently scheduled, there is no threat of imminent,
> irreparable harm."

The court's own footnote 12 distinguished *Crawford* on the ground that these Plaintiffs
**do** plead a wrongful-foreclosure claim.

**Doc 41 (2026-09-15, Notice of Foreclosure Sale Scheduling) appears to negate the sole
stated basis for that denial.** A denial as moot is not a merits denial. If a sale is now
scheduled, the mootness premise is gone. This is the strongest available development in
the case and it is currently unexploited.
