# TACTICAL FRAME — items 03 and 06
*Applied method, with drafted language. Audience: Magistrate Judge Howard, then Judge
Story. Written-motion practice only.*

## The governing reframe

Defendants' vocabulary, taken from their own filings, builds one frame: **a defaulting
borrower trying to stop a lawful foreclosure.** Doc 37-5 at 2: "the severity of the
delinquency," "we have been unable to determine that any error has occurred."

Accepting that frame loses. Every paper should carry the competing frame: **a servicer
foreclosing on a balance its own records do not establish.**

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "we fell behind" / "the default" | "the balance Defendants assert" |
| "Shellpoint says we owe" | "Defendants' own ledger states" |
| "they lost our payment" | "the May 17, 2025 payment carries no reversal and no NSF fee" |
| "wrongful" / "fraudulent" / "egregious" | the figure, the date, the page cite |

Adjectives signal that the number is weak. The number is not weak. Let it carry.

## 1. Deep Issue — Motion for Leave (item 03), opening paragraph

Garner's premise–premise–question form, under 75 words, placed before any background:

> Rule 15(a)(2) directs that leave to amend be freely given when justice so requires, and
> denial requires a substantial reason. Plaintiffs omitted their foreclosure-related claims
> on August 21, 2026, when no sale was scheduled. On September 15, 2026, Defendants
> scheduled one for December 1. Should Plaintiffs be denied leave to replead claims whose
> sole reason for omission was a fact Defendants themselves reversed twenty-five days later?

The question answers itself, which is the point. It also silently supplies the Rule 15
standard and the only two dates that matter.

## 2. Deep Issue — Renewed PI Motion (item 06), replacing the current opening

> This Court denied Plaintiffs' prior injunction motion as moot, on the sole ground that no
> foreclosure sale was scheduled. Defendants have now scheduled one for December 1, 2026.
> Their own filed ledger shows $1,899.56 of Plaintiffs' money held unapplied since
> May 17, 2025, while they disbursed approximately $5,800 to foreclose. Should the home be
> sold before the Court determines what is actually owed?

Keep the existing block quote of Doc 34 at 25 immediately beneath it. The syllogism
anchors; the quotation proves the major premise in the Court's own words.

## 3. Stealing Thunder — already deployed once; deploy it again

The PI motion's Part IV does this correctly: it names the Georgia tender rule as "the most
substantial obstacle to the relief they seek," cites the authority *against* Plaintiffs
(*Brevard*, *Sparra*, *Sheely*), and then resolves it by offering tender as a condition.
That is the single strongest passage in the package. Do not soften it.

**The Rule 15 motion must do the same thing with Doc 35.** Howard wrote that Plaintiffs
"might not be given another opportunity to amend." She will remember. Raising it first,
in your own voice, is worth more than any argument made after she raises it:

> Plaintiffs do not minimize this Court's caution that they "might not be given another
> opportunity to amend their pleadings." (Doc. 35 at 8.) Plaintiffs took that caution
> seriously: the August 21 pleading they filed in response to it abandoned five causes of
> action, dropped three Defendants, and complied with every structural requirement the
> Order imposed. Plaintiffs do not seek leave because they were careless with the
> opportunity the Court gave them. They seek leave because, twenty-five days after they
> narrowed the pleading on the premise that no sale was scheduled, Defendants scheduled
> one.

This converts the Court's warning from a weapon against the motion into evidence of
compliance.

## 4. Correcting the record — Motion for Leave, immediately after the Deep Issue

> Plaintiffs note one correction to the record. Plaintiffs' Response in Opposition to
> Doc. 40, filed September 22, 2026, stated that a Motion for Leave to File a Second
> Amended Complaint was being filed concurrently with that Response. Through Plaintiffs'
> inadvertence it was not; Plaintiffs prepared this Motion on September 22, 2026 but filed
> only their responses to the pending motions to dismiss on that date. Plaintiffs file this
> Motion now and bring the error to the Court's attention at the first opportunity. No
> Defendant has responded to Doc. 40 in reliance on that statement, and Plaintiffs are
> aware of no resulting prejudice. If any Defendant contends otherwise, Plaintiffs do not
> oppose a reasonable extension of any deadline affected.

Same mechanism. Self-reported reads as diligence; found by Anderson reads as carelessness.

## 5. Attacking Defendants' major premise

Defendants' Rule 12 syllogism (Doc 37-1 at 16–18) runs: *a complaint pleading
"conditionally" — that a breach exists "to the extent" an unauthorized charge exists —
pleads nothing.* Their minor premise is that Doc 36 pleads only conditionally.

Do not argue about what "conditional" means. **Attack the minor premise with the ledger.**
On a Rule 65 motion the Court takes evidence, and the $1,899.56 is not conditional — it is
printed on their own exhibit, under their own authentication, in a document they ask the
Court to credit. The PI motion already makes this move at Part III. It is the strongest
structural play in the package because it uses their chosen evidence against their chosen
framing.

## 6. Uniqueness — close the alternative explanation

The one gap an opponent will drive at: *why is there one unreversed payment among four?*
If Defendants supply an innocent explanation and Plaintiffs have none, the finding loses
its force. Address it before they do:

> Three of the four payments carry a reversal flag, a matching code-2 reversal, and an NSF
> fee the same day. The May 17 payment carries none of the three. Defendants have offered
> no explanation for the difference, and the only explanation their own records support is
> that the payment cleared and was never applied.

## 7. What to leave out

No anaphora. No metonymy. No "crusade." No characterization of Defendants' motives beyond
what the ledger shows. No adverbs on the accounting findings. Howard's July 31 order is a
precise, unrhetorical document and it rewards papers written the same way.
