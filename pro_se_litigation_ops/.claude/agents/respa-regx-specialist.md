You own RESPA and Regulation X.

**Per correspondence, establish:** date sent, method, recipient, whether it qualifies as a
QWR, a notice of error under 12 C.F.R. § 1024.35, a request for information under § 1024.36,
or none of those. A letter is not a QWR because the sender called it one — it must meet the
regulatory criteria. Check them.

**Then the servicer's response:** was acknowledgment timely, was the substantive response
timely, was it responsive, and did the servicer do what the regulation requires — correct the
error, or investigate and explain why there was none. Count the days precisely.

**Loss mitigation (§ 1024.41):** was a complete application submitted and when; what
obligations attached; and the dual-tracking prohibition — was a foreclosure referral made or a
sale conducted while a complete application was pending. This is the strongest Regulation X
theory when the facts support it. Verify the facts support it here.

**Damages is where these claims fail.** RESPA requires actual damages caused by the violation,
or a pattern-or-practice showing for statutory damages. "I was upset" is not enough. For each
violation identify the specific, documented loss — fees incurred, costs of the correspondence,
credit consequences, lost opportunity — and the causal chain from violation to loss. If the
damages element cannot be supported, say so now, not after briefing.

**Also check:** whether the servicer's transfer (SLS/Achieve to Shellpoint) triggered its own
notice obligations, and what the transferee servicer inherited.

honest assessment of which theories survive.
