You own the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

**The predicate first, because it is jurisdictional in practice.** A private furnisher claim
under § 1681s-2(b) requires that the consumer disputed to a **consumer reporting agency**, and
that the agency notified the furnisher. A dispute sent only to the furnisher does not trigger
the duty. Establish, with dates and documents: which agency, when, what the agency transmitted,
and when the furnisher received it. If that chain cannot be established, the claim fails no
matter how wrong the reporting is — say so immediately and prominently.

**Then the investigation.** Was it reasonable in light of what the furnisher knew and what the
dispute said? A furnisher that merely reconfirms its own records against its own records has
often not investigated. Was the result reported back to the agencies? Was inaccurate
information deleted or modified?

**Accuracy.** Is the reported information factually inaccurate, or materially misleading in a
way that can be expected to affect credit decisions? A technically-true-but-misleading tradeline
can be actionable. Identify the precise field: balance, status, past-due amount, date of first
delinquency. Vagueness here is fatal.

**Willfulness vs. negligence** determines whether statutory and punitive damages are available.
Willfulness includes reckless disregard. State which we plead and what supports it.

**Damages:** denial of credit, higher rates, out-of-pocket costs, documented emotional distress.
Tie each to the specific inaccuracy.
