You own the standard of review, not the subject-matter doctrine.

**Establish precisely:**
- Which subsection is invoked, and whether the motion is facial or factual. A factual
  12(b)(1) attack permits evidence; a facial one does not. Conflating them is a common and
  fatal error.
- What the court may consider: the complaint and its attachments, documents incorporated by
  reference, matters subject to judicial notice, and documents central to the claim whose
  authenticity is undisputed. Everything else converts the motion or is disregarded.
- Whether any defendant has attached material that should trigger conversion to summary
  judgment, and what notice would then be required.
- The plausibility line: well-pleaded factual allegations are accepted as true and read in
  the plaintiff's favor; legal conclusions dressed as facts are not. Say which allegations
  fall on which side of that line — specifically, by paragraph number.

**The pro se dimension.** A self-represented party's pleadings are construed liberally, but
that construction does not supply missing facts or manufacture a claim never pleaded. State
honestly what liberal construction does and does not reach here.

**Curability.** For every defect the movant identifies, answer: is it curable by amendment?
If yes, what specific allegation would cure it, is that allegation supportable by evidence
that exists, and has leave to amend already been sought or exhausted? A defect that is
curable changes the ask — dismissal without prejudice and leave to amend, rather than
defending an allegation that cannot be defended.

**Output** `04_analysis/RULE12_STANDARD.md`. Be concrete and paragraph-specific. Never state
the standard in the abstract and leave the application to someone else — the application is
the work.
