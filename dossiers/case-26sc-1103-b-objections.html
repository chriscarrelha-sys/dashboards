Before you rely on this

Every citation here is to Georgia’s 2013 Evidence Code (O.C.G.A. Title 24) or to Rule 56. The propositions are stated as the statutes read. Pull each section and read it yourself — a wrong subsection number said out loud costs you more than saying nothing.

Rankin v. Smith is named without a reporter citation on purpose. I have not been able to verify the volume and page. The underlying principle — that counsel’s statements are not evidence — is settled and you can argue it without naming a case. Verify the cite before you say it aloud, or don’t say it.

An objection you cannot state in one breath is an objection you will not make in time. Learn the short forms in § 03 first; the reasoning in §§ 01–02 is for when the Court asks you to explain.

01

Objections you make

State the objection in three words, then stop. Give the ground only if the Court asks. The first four are the ones that carry this motion.

Lack of personal knowledge

O.C.G.A. § 24-6-602 · § 9-11-56(e)

When
The affidavit asserts a fact the affiant could only know from reading the file — balances, what was applied where, why an entry was made.
Say
“Objection, Your Honor — lack of personal knowledge.”
If asked to explain
A witness may testify to a matter only if evidence is introduced sufficient to support a finding of personal knowledge. Rule 56(e) goes further and requires the affidavit to show that competence affirmatively, on its face. Review of a record is not personal knowledge of the events the record describes.

Foundation — business records

O.C.G.A. § 24-8-803(6)

When
Records are offered before the four foundation elements are established: made at or near the time, by or from a person with knowledge, kept in the regular course, and regular practice to make.
Say
“Objection — no foundation. The 803(6) elements haven’t been established.”
If asked to explain
The burden of laying the foundation is on the proponent. Until all four elements are shown by the custodian or another qualified witness, the exception does not open and the records remain hearsay.

Trustworthiness — the 803(6) escape clause

O.C.G.A. § 24-8-803(6), final clause

When
The foundation elements are technically met but the record itself shows post hoc alteration — the reversals, the core-conversion adjustment, the inserted fees.
Say
“Objection, Your Honor. Even if the foundation is laid, the circumstances of preparation indicate a lack of trustworthiness.”
If asked to explain
The exception applies unless the source of information or the method or circumstances of preparation indicate lack of trustworthiness. Point to a specific entry on a specific page — the October 23, 2025 entry commented “adjustment related to core conversion” is the cleanest.
Note
This is the strongest evidentiary objection in the case. It survives even if the Court finds Farr competent and the foundation adequate.

Hearsay

O.C.G.A. § 24-8-801 · § 24-8-802

When
An out-of-court statement is offered for its truth and no exception has been established.
Say
“Objection — hearsay.”
If asked to explain
Name the statement, say it is offered for its truth, and say no exception has been laid. Do not argue the exception for them.

Conclusory / legal conclusion

O.C.G.A. § 9-11-56(e)

When
The affidavit says “Defendants defaulted,” “the amount due and owing is,” or “the account is in default” without the facts underneath.
Say
“Objection — that’s a legal conclusion, not a fact admissible in evidence.”
If asked to explain
Rule 56(e) requires the affidavit to set forth facts that would be admissible. A conclusion of law is not such a fact, and an affidavit made of conclusions is not cured by the affiant’s employment.

Best evidence

O.C.G.A. § 24-10-1002

When
A witness or affidavit characterizes what a document says instead of putting the document in front of the Court.
Say
“Objection — best evidence. If the document says that, the document should be before the Court.”
Note
Narrower than it sounds — it applies to proving the content of a writing, not to every mention of one. Use it sparingly so it lands when you do.

Authentication

O.C.G.A. § 24-9-901

When
An exhibit appears that no one has connected to its source — an unsigned printout, a screenshot, a summary with no underlying record.
Say
“Objection — not authenticated.”
If asked to explain
The proponent must produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what it is claimed to be. Nothing before the Court does that here.

Counsel is testifying

Rankin v. Smith — verify cite before naming

When
Opposing counsel supplies a fact from the podium that appears in no affidavit and no exhibit. Expect this on the payment history.
Say
“Objection, Your Honor. Counsel is testifying. That fact isn’t in the affidavit or in any exhibit before the Court.”
Note
Make it on the principle, not the citation. Then ask that the statement be disregarded — the ask is what makes it a ruling instead of a complaint.
02

What Regions will say — and your answer

The five you should expect. The first two decide the motion; prepare those cold.

“That goes to weight, not admissibility.”

The argument most likely to sink this motion

Answer
“Your Honor, weight is the analysis after evidence is admitted. Rule 56(e) is a threshold: an affidavit that doesn’t show personal knowledge on its face isn’t weak evidence, it isn’t evidence the Court may consider at all. And 803(6) is written the same way — the trustworthiness clause is a condition of admission, not a comment on persuasiveness.”
Then
Return immediately to the four corners of the affidavit. Do not let the argument drift to whether the debt is owed.

“The CFPB response is hearsay.”

O.C.G.A. § 24-8-801(d)(2)

Answer
“It’s Regions’ own statement, offered against Regions. That’s an admission by a party opponent under 801(d)(2) — by definition not hearsay. Regions wrote it, to its own regulator, about this account.”
Note
Know this one cold. Tab 04 is your best exhibit and this is the only serious objection to it. A statement by a party, or by the party’s agent concerning a matter within the scope of the agency, offered against that party, is excluded from the definition of hearsay.

“A custodian need not have made the entries” — citing Windham

Windham v. State, 344 Ga. App. 721 — verify

Answer
“We agree with that proposition, Your Honor, and it isn’t our argument. Windham is a criminal case in which records came in at trial after a live witness laid the foundation and was cross-examined. This is a paper affidavit offered without the affiant on a Rule 56 motion. Our objection is that this affidavit doesn’t make the affirmative showing 9-11-56(e) requires.”
Note
Conceding the general proposition costs nothing and buys credibility for the narrow ground.

“Argumentative” / “asked and answered” on cross

Form objections

Answer
Don’t argue it. Rephrase shorter and ask again. If it is genuinely the same question because the witness didn’t answer: “Your Honor, the witness hasn’t answered. I’ll rephrase.” Then rephrase.
Note
Winning a form fight gains you nothing. Getting the admission gains you everything.

“The motion is untimely” or “this should have been raised in a response brief”

Procedural

Answer
“Your Honor, an objection to the admissibility of summary judgment evidence is properly raised by motion to strike, and the Court has to satisfy itself that the evidence it relies on is admissible in any event. If the Court would prefer it briefed, we’ll brief it.”
Then
Never fight the judge on procedure. Offer to cure and move back to substance.

Two habits that matter more than any objection on this page. Say the word “objection” before you have finished deciding which one — it stops the record and buys you two seconds. And after the Court rules, say “thank you, Your Honor” and move on, whichever way it went.
