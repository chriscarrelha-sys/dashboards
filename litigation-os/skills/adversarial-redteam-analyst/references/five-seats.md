# The Five Seats

Each seat is a different reader with different incentives and a different
vocabulary of objection. Working them separately is what produces coverage;
merging them produces a generic critique that misses the specific.

---

## Seat 1 — Defense counsel

**Incentive:** dispose of the case cheaply, or narrow it until it is not worth
trying. Paid by the hour but judged on outcomes.

**What this seat is uniquely positioned to catch:** the cheapest path to
disposition, and the plaintiff's own words used against them.

**Prompts:**
- Which claims die on the face of the pleading, with no evidence at all?
- Which defendant can I remove entirely? Removing a party is worth more than
  narrowing a claim.
- What has the plaintiff **admitted**? A pleading is a judicial admission of the
  facts it states, and plaintiffs routinely plead facts that establish an element
  of a defense — a date that starts limitations, an acknowledgment of default, a
  concession that a payment failed.
- Which of the plaintiff's **own exhibits** contradicts the allegations that cite
  them? An exhibit attached to a pleading generally controls over a contrary
  allegation, and this is the cheapest kill available.
- Where is the damages theory speculative? Lost employment, lost opportunity, and
  emotional distress are the usual soft spots, and often the largest numbers.
- What is unpleaded that the statute requires — notice, exhaustion, a condition
  precedent, a specific dispute channel?
- Where can I demand particularity — fraud, and anything sounding in fraud?
- What does delay get me? If the plaintiff needs an injunction and cannot get one,
  time is my ally.
- Is any theory on the *rejected theories* list still in the pleading? If so, I
  lead with it, because it makes everything else look weaker.

## Seat 2 — A skeptical magistrate judge

**Incentive:** move the docket, apply the rules evenly, avoid reversal. Sees
volume; screens pro se filings; has already issued orders in this case.

**What this seat is uniquely positioned to catch:** non-compliance with the
court's own prior orders, and the difference between a pleading that looks
compliant and one that is.

**Prompts:**
- Does this filing do what my last order told them to do? Read the order's words
  and check each requirement, one by one.
- Am I being asked to hunt through the record to find the claim? That is the core
  shotgun-pleading objection and it survives redrafting if the substance has not
  changed.
- This is the second attempt. Did they use it well, or produce a longer version of
  the same problem?
- Which parts can I dispose of without reaching anything hard?
- Is the length proportionate? A pleading that grows substantially after an order
  to make it *shorter and clearer* reads badly before a word of it is considered.
- Are they treating my leave to replead as leave to do other things — add parties,
  add claims — that I did not authorize?
- Is anything sanctionable, or merely poor? Pro se status earns liberal
  construction, not exemption from the rules.

## Seat 3 — The assigned district judge

**Incentive:** a trial-ready case or a clean disposition. Reviews the magistrate's
recommendations; owns the calendar.

**What this seat is uniquely positioned to catch:** whether the case is actually
going somewhere, and whether the parties are managing themselves.

**Prompts:**
- After the pending motions, what is left, and is it triable?
- Are these theories ones that survive summary judgment, or only dismissal?
  Surviving a motion to dismiss on a theory with no evidence behind it postpones
  the problem; it does not solve it.
- Is a party asking me to run a document fight I should not be running?
- What is the realistic disposition — settlement, summary judgment, trial — and
  what is the timeline?
- Has this party been reasonable? That assessment, formed early, colours
  discretionary rulings for the rest of the case.
- Have I already ruled on something they are re-arguing?

## Seat 4 — Evidentiary and Rule 11 review

**Incentive:** none — this seat is a mirror. It is the most uncomfortable and the
most valuable.

**What this seat is uniquely positioned to catch:** allegations that cannot be
supported, and the gap between what we believe and what we can show.

**The core test, applied per factual contention:** does it have evidentiary
support, or is it specifically identified as likely to have support after a
reasonable opportunity for investigation? "We are confident" is not a basis.
"Based on Plaintiffs' personal knowledge" is a basis **if** the plaintiff has
personal knowledge of that fact — a borrower has personal knowledge of what they
paid, and does not have personal knowledge of what a servicer's system did.

**Prompts:**
- For each material factual contention, what is the support, by source ID?
- Which allegations rest on "information and belief"? Is the basis for the belief
  stated? Is the information peculiarly within the other side's control, which is
  when that formulation is appropriate?
- Which **numbers** have no document behind them? Damages figures, balances,
  amounts claimed to have been paid. Numbers attract scrutiny.
- Do any of our exhibits impeach our own allegations?
- Are we asserting a legal position that has been rejected in this forum without
  arguing for a change in the law?
- If sanctions were sought on any paragraph, what would our answer be? If we do
  not have one, that paragraph needs to change.
- Is there an allegation we removed from an earlier draft? If it came out because
  it could not be supported, make sure it has not crept back.

**Rate:** `none` / `low` / `material` / `high`, per paragraph. Anything at
`material` or above is reported in the short conclusion, not buried.

## Seat 5 — Appellate preservation

**Incentive:** a reviewable record. Reads the record cold, two years later.

**What this seat is uniquely positioned to catch:** things that are invisible
today and fatal later.

**Prompts:**
- What argument has been **waived** by not raising it at the first opportunity?
- What was raised but **not preserved** — argued in passing, no ruling obtained,
  no objection on the record?
- Is there a ruling to appeal from? Is it final, or would an appeal be premature?
- Was each argument made in the right document at the right time? An argument
  raised first in a reply, or first in an objection to a recommendation, is
  commonly treated as forfeited.
- If claims are dismissed, **with or without prejudice** — and does that
  distinction matter for state-law claims that might be refiled elsewhere?
- What needs to be in the record **now** because it cannot be added later?
- Has any factual concession been made that will bind us on appeal?
- If we lose the pending motion, what is the standard of review, and does that
  change what we should be doing today?

## Common blind spots across all five seats

Check these explicitly; they hide between seats:

- **Our own prior filings.** Positions taken earlier that conflict with positions
  taken now. The other side reads the whole docket.
- **Relief that does not match the injury.** A remedy nobody can grant on the
  facts pleaded.
- **The second-strongest defendant.** Attention concentrates on the main target
  while a weaker claim against another defendant goes unexamined and becomes the
  first thing dismissed.
- **Arguments that win the motion and lose the case.** A theory that survives
  dismissal but commits us to proving something unprovable.
- **The unforced error.** A rejected theory, an unsupported number, an
  inflammatory characterisation — each individually survivable, collectively
  expensive, and all of them free to remove.
