# Extraction Protocol by Document Type

Each document type proves a characteristic thing and hides a characteristic trap.
Read the relevant section before extracting from a type you have not handled in
this matter.

---

## Pleadings (complaints, answers, counterclaims)

**Proves:** what a party has formally asserted and is bound by; what is admitted.

**Extract:** each numbered allegation with its ¶ number; the counts and what each
is asserted against whom; admissions and denials in an answer, paragraph by
paragraph; affirmative defenses; the prayer for relief; verification and
signature blocks.

**Traps:**
- Everything in a complaint is `[ALLEGED]`, including your own client's
  allegations. A verified complaint is still an allegation; verification affects
  its evidentiary use, not its epistemic status.
- An answer's admissions are judicial admissions and are worth far more than
  anything in the complaint. Extract them specifically.
- **Paragraph numbering that restarts by section** makes cross-references
  ("realleges ¶¶ 1–46") ambiguous. When a pleading has several paragraph 1s,
  record that as a defect and note which ¶ 1 you mean in every pinpoint.
- Signature and verification blocks left as template placeholders (`/s/`,
  "this ___ day of") mean the document as supplied is unexecuted. Record it.
- A count asserted against a party named only in the caption, with no factual
  allegations directed at it, is a notice defect worth flagging.

## Court orders and opinions

**Proves:** what the court decided and ordered; occasionally, facts found.

**Extract:** the docket number and date; the issuing judge; the operative
language **verbatim** (the words that command, deny, direct, or set a date); the
disposition of each motion; every deadline stated; any condition attached to
future conduct.

**Traps:**
- **Recitation is not finding.** "Plaintiffs allege X" and "The gist of
  Plaintiffs' suit is X" are recitations. "The Court finds X" and "It is ORDERED
  that X" are the court speaking. Label accordingly — this is the highest-value
  distinction in the whole protocol.
- A footnote reciting background supplied by one side is that side's account,
  even when it reads as neutral narrative.
- "Denied as moot" is not "denied on the merits" and does not foreclose the
  argument.
- A magistrate judge's order, a report and recommendation, and a district
  judge's order have different effects and different review paths. Record which.
- Deadlines stated in an order are express; anything you compute from the order
  is calculated and belongs to the docket specialist with its method shown.

## Correspondence (letters, emails, notices)

**Proves:** that the statement was made, by whom, when, and to whom. Not that
the statement is true.

**Extract:** sender, recipient, date on the document, date received if shown,
delivery method, tracking or reference numbers, subject/reference lines, account
numbers, the operative assertions, enclosures listed.

**Traps:**
- An enclosure list naming documents not in the file is direct evidence of a
  missing-evidence row.
- A servicer's or insurer's statement that "no error occurred" is a
  representation, not a fact about errors.
- Form letters recycle boilerplate; the same paragraph appearing in five letters
  is not five independent confirmations.
- Date on the letter, postmark, and receipt can all differ and can all matter.
- A letter describing an earlier event is a source for that earlier event at its
  own date, not at the letter's date.

## Account records, ledgers, and payment histories

**Proves:** what the institution's system recorded. Not what actually happened
with the money, and not that the entries are correct.

**Extract, per line:** transaction date, effective date, due date applied to,
transaction description, internal codes, amount, allocation to
principal/interest/escrow/fees/suspense, resulting balances, and the running
balance. Preserve the row number.

**Traps:**
- Reversals often appear as separate later rows, not as edits. A payment posted
  and reversed looks like two events, and the net effect must be computed and
  shown, not assumed.
- Negative amounts can mean a reversal, a credit, a correction, or a sign
  convention — determine which from the balance movement, and mark
  `[UNRESOLVED]` if the record does not say.
- A balance appearing on an origination-date row (a late-charge balance on day
  one, for instance) is a genuine anomaly worth recording — but record it as
  *what the ledger shows*, not as proof of what caused it.
- Suspense and "unapplied" balances are the single most common locus of
  servicing disputes. Trace money into and out of suspense line by line.
- **OCR on tabular financial data is unreliable.** Column misalignment silently
  moves a figure into the wrong field. Spot-check that debits, credits, and the
  running balance reconcile; where they do not, say the reconciliation fails
  rather than adjusting a figure.
- Two identifiers for one account after a transfer is ordinary, not a
  contradiction — unless a party has treated them as different obligations.

## Assignments, security instruments, and recorded documents

**Proves:** what was recorded, when, and what the instrument recites.

**Extract:** instrument type; execution date; **recording** date; book and page
or instrument number; grantor and grantee exactly as written; any nominee or
capacity language; the property description; signatures, notarization, and
witnesses; every recital.

**Traps:**
- Execution date, effective date, and recording date are three facts. Keep them
  apart; deadlines and priority can turn on which one applies.
- Capacity language ("as nominee for X") is a recital, not proof of the
  relationship it recites. Whether the recital is accurate is a separate
  question requiring separate evidence.
- A recorded instrument proves recording. It does not prove the underlying
  transfer occurred, was authorized, or was effective.
- Compare the instrument's recitals against the other documents in the file —
  dates and party names in recitals are a frequent source of genuine conflicts.

## Credit reports and furnishing records

**Proves:** what a bureau displayed on a given date and what a furnisher
reported.

**Extract:** report date; the specific tradeline; every field (balance, credit
limit, past-due amount, status code, payment history grid, date of first
delinquency, date reported); which bureau; dispute flags; and the exact field
values as displayed.

**Traps:**
- Different bureaus show different data for the same account; that is normal and
  is not by itself a contradiction. An internal impossibility within one report —
  a zero balance with an amount past due, a balance above the credit limit on a
  closed line — is a different and much stronger finding.
- A report shows a snapshot. It does not show when the data was furnished or by
  whom, unless the report says so.
- Payment-history grids use per-bureau coding conventions; read the legend rather
  than assuming.

## Declarations and affidavits

**Proves:** what the declarant swore, and what they claim personal knowledge of.

**Extract:** declarant name and title; the personal-knowledge paragraph; each
factual assertion with its ¶; every document referenced; the execution date and
place; the penalty-of-perjury language.

**Traps:**
- A declaration "based on review of business records" is not personal knowledge
  of the underlying events; note the difference, it bears on admissibility.
- Assertions beyond the stated scope of knowledge are vulnerable.
- Compare declarations by the same declarant across filings, and against the
  business records they describe. Divergence there is high-value.

---

## Agency and regulator records (complaint portals, investigations)

**Proves:** what was submitted, what the company responded, and the file's
administrative status.

**Extract:** complaint/reference number; submission date; the consumer's
narrative; the company's response and its date; the closure category; any
enclosures the response lists.

**Traps:**
- "Closed with explanation" is an administrative status, not an adjudication.
- A company's portal response is a party statement and may be a party admission —
  extract its concessions precisely and quote them.
- Portal submission is not necessarily delivery to a statutorily designated
  address; whether it counts as notice is a legal question, not a factual one.
  Flag it for the research specialist rather than resolving it.

---

## Transcripts

**Proves:** what was said on the record.

**Extract:** page and line for every quotation; the speaker; the question the
answer responds to.

**Traps:** an answer read apart from its question frequently reverses meaning;
"objection, form" preserves an issue that may exclude the testimony; corrections
on an errata sheet change the record and must be checked for.
