--- [Doc. 34 at 3 n.4] (court's background recitation) ---
"The subject HELOC Loan was originated in July 2022 by Lendage, LLC, in the
amount of $149,650.00. To secure repayment, Plaintiffs executed a Security Deed
conveying the Property to Mortgage Electronic Registration Systems, Inc. ('MERS')
as nominee for Lendage, LLC. In 2024, MERS transferred interest in the Security
Deed to MEB Loan Trust VIII ('MEB'). Specialized Loan Servicing, LLC ('SLS') was
the loan servicer for MEB. In February 2024, once Plaintiffs fell behind on
monthly payments, SLS wrote to Plaintiffs concerning the default. SLS initiated
non-judicial foreclosure proceedings against the Property pursuant to the terms
of the Security Deed, on behalf of MEB, as the holder of the Security Deed, and
SLS, as the servicer of the Loan. On or about June 20, 2024, the loan servicer of
the HELOC was transferred from SLS to Shellpoint Mortgage Servicing
('Shellpoint'), the current servicer. [See Amended Notice of Removal, Exhibit C
(SLS merged with NewRez, LLC d/b/a Shellpoint Mortgage Servicing)]. Loss
mitigation efforts explored between Shellpoint and Plaintiffs dating back to
August 2024 have been unsuccessful. Since that time, several foreclosure sales
have been scheduled and postponed. In July 2025, Shellpoint retained McCalla
Raymer Leibert Pierce, LLP to conduct the non-judicial foreclosure."

--- [Doc. 34 at 4] ---
"The gist of Plaintiffs' suit is that Defendants improperly applied funds to the
HELOC Loan, giving rise to violations of state and federal law. According to the
removing Defendants, the HELOC Loan has been in default since January 2024.
Foreclosure was scheduled to occur on March 3, 2026, but was voluntarily
postponed by Shellpoint. [Dkt. 27 - Resp. to Pls. Mot. PI, at 6; Alsaadi Decl.,
para. 14]. Defendants plan to notice the Property for a foreclosure sale in the
future in the event the parties are unable to resolve the matter. [Resp. to Pls.
Mot. PI, at 6]."

--- [Doc. 34 at 5] (claims in the original Verified Complaint) ---
"Plaintiffs' Verified Complaint asserts claims alleging violations of the Real
Estate Settlement Procedures Act ('RESPA'), 12 U.S.C. Sec. 2605, 12 C.F.R. Secs.
1024.35, 1024.36, 1024.41, against Shellpoint and SLS (Count I); violations of the
Fair Credit Reporting Act ('FCRA'), 15 U.S.C. Secs. 1681, et seq. (Count II);
violations of the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act ('FDCPA'), 15 U.S.C. Secs.
1692, et seq. (Count III); Wrongful Foreclosure, O.C.G.A. Secs. 44-14-162, et
seq., 23-2-114, against all Defendants (Count IV); Breach of Contract against
Shellpoint and MEB Trust (Count V); and Declaratory Judgment against all
Defendants, O.C.G.A. Sec. 9-4-2 (Count VI)."

--- [Doc. 34 at 6-7] (state-court history) ---
"On March 3, 2026, Chief Judge Bagley issued a Rule Nisi Order denying Plaintiffs'
Motion for a Temporary Restraining Order ('TRO') and setting an interlocutory
injunction hearing for his two-day calendar beginning March 5, 2026. [Dkt. 26-1,
at 102-105 - 3/3/26 Order and Rule Nisi]. In denying Plaintiffs' Motion for TRO,
Judge Bagley found that Plaintiffs were unable to show irreparable harm in that,
even if the foreclosure proceeded, Plaintiffs had adequate remedies at law,
namely, seeking to set aside foreclosure or seeking damages for wrongful
foreclosure. [Id., at 104]."

--- [Doc. 34 at 7] (the funding-evidence finding) ---
"On March 6, 2026, Judge Bagley issued an Order finding that 'an immediate or
eminent threat of nonjudicial foreclosure does not exist' as the March 3
foreclosure was withdrawn and would not be rescheduled prior to May 2026. [Id.].
Judge Bagley explained that the court would require additional evidence to
determine whether Plaintiffs were likely to succeed on the merits of their
underlying claims. [Id.]. Specifically, Judge Bagley stated that 'many of the
Plaintiffs' claims are contingent upon whether HELOC loan proceeds were actually
delivered by the lender.' [Id.]. Judge Bagley further explained that, if HELOC
loan proceeds were delivered, the court would need to know the dates of delivery
and the value of loan proceeds delivered to Plaintiffs and/or Plaintiffs'
creditors. [Id.]."

--- [Doc. 34 at 14] (remand grounds raised) ---
"(1) Whether this federal district court is precluded from exercising concurrent
jurisdiction over the Property under the 'prior-exclusive jurisdiction' doctrine;
(2) Whether the Notice of Removal violates the unanimity rule of 28 U.S.C. Sec.
1446(b)(2)(A) since Lendage, LLC was served March 17, 2026 and did not join or
consent to removal; and (3) Whether the unanimity defect is uncurable given that
Lendage, LLC's thirty-day consent window under 28 U.S.C. Sec. 1446(b)(2)(B) has
expired."

--- [Doc. 34 at 20] (prior exclusive jurisdiction holding) ---
"For these reasons, the Court finds that Plaintiffs' prior exclusive jurisdiction
argument lacks merit and does not require remand."

--- [Doc. 34 at 21] (Lendage party status) ---
"As an initial matter, Lendage, LLC was not, and is not, a named defendant in
Plaintiffs' Verified Complaint. [Resp. in Opp'n to Remand, at 10 (citing V.
Compl., at 1 - Case Caption)]. And Defendants correctly state that Lendage was
never recognized as a defendant by the Superior Court of Forsyth County prior to
removal."
"In post-removal filings, Plaintiffs add 'Lendage, LLC' or 'Lendage, LLC d/b/a' to
the case caption or certificates of service with their filings but Plaintiffs
never moved to amend their Verified Complaint to add Lendage, LLC as a party-
defendant."

--- [Doc. 34 at 22 n.11] ---
"The failure to name Lendage, LLC is more than a scrivener's error. Lendage, LLC
is only mentioned within Plaintiffs' Verified Complaint as the originator of the
HELOC Loan but not otherwise identified within the causes of action as an entity
purportedly in violation of the statutes relied upon. [V. Compl., paras. 15-22].
Therefore, liberally construing the allegations advanced by the Carrelhas, their
grievances are not directed at Lendage, LLC, but rather the actions of SLS and
Shellpoint, the entities that serviced the HELOC Loan after origination."

--- [Doc. 34 at 23] (unanimity holding) ---
"Moreover, even if the unanimity rule was not complied with, this would not defeat
jurisdiction. See Goss v. Aetna, Inc., 360 F. Supp. 3d 1364, 1370 (N.D. Ga. 2019)
(quoting Hernandez v. Seminole Cty., 334 F.3d 1233, 1236 (11th Cir. 2003)). The
Eleventh Circuit has expressly held that 'failure to comply with the unanimity
requirement is only a procedural defect, not a jurisdictional defect.' Id. (citing
Stone v. Bank of N.Y. Mellon, N.A., 609 F. App'x 979, 981-82 (11th Cir. 2015))."

--- [Doc. 34 at 24-25] (findings) ---
"The Court finds that the Court has federal subject matter jurisdiction, that the
prior exclusive jurisdiction doctrine has no application to these facts, that
Lendage, LLC is not named as a defendant, and that any purported violation of the
unanimity rule does not require or warrant remand."
"Lastly, Plaintiffs' request for a preliminary injunction is denied as moot given
Defendants' representation that the foreclosure sale that precipitated Plaintiffs'
motion has been postponed indefinitely."
"With no foreclosure sale currently scheduled, there is no threat of imminent,
irreparable harm."
