--- [Doc. 35 at 5] (shotgun pleading — standard) ---
"'[T]he court must instruct a party - whether a plaintiff or defendant - to replead
when it files any one of four "shotgun pleadings."' Dickerson v. Biden, No.
1:24-CV-04457-SCJ, 2025 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 229938, at *3 (N.D. Ga. Jan. 14, 2025)
(citing Est. of Bass v. Regions Bank, Inc., 947 F.3d 1352, 1358 (11th Cir. 2020)
and Jackson v. Bank of Am., N.A., 898 F.3d 1348, 1357 (11th Cir. 2018))."

--- [Doc. 35 at 5-6] (the four types, quoting Weiland) ---
"'The first, and most common, is "a complaint containing multiple counts where each
count adopts the allegations of all preceding counts, causing each successive count
to carry all that came before and the last count to be a combination of the entire
complaint."' Id. at *3-4 (quoting Weiland v. Palm Beach Cnty. Sheriff's Off., 792
F.3d 1313, 1321 (11th Cir. 2015)). 'The second, "a complaint . . . replete with
conclusory, vague, and immaterial facts not obviously connected to any particular
cause of action."' Id. at *4 (quoting Weiland, 792 F.3d at 1322). 'The third, a
complaint "not separating into a different count each cause of action or claim for
relief."' Id. (quoting Weiland, 792 F.3d at 1323). 'And the fourth, a complaint with
"multiple claims against multiple defendants without specifying which of the
defendants are responsible for which acts or omissions, or which of the defendants
the claim is brought against."' Id. (quoting Weiland, 792 F.3d at 1323)."

--- [Doc. 35 at 7] (holding as to this complaint) ---
"Here, Plaintiffs assert 92 paragraphs of factual allegations and then each of their
seven courts 'reallege[s] and incorporate[s] by reference each and every allegation
contained in the preceding paragraphs as though fully set forth herein,' thus
incorporating all paragraphs of each preceding Count into each subsequent Count. See
Doc. 26-1 at 18-29, paras. 93, 103, 111, 122, 127, 133, 138. This is the first type
of shotgun pleading described above, and it is improper. Weiland, 792 F.3d at 1321.
By incorporating each preceding Count into each following Count, Plaintiffs'
Complaint fails 'to give the defendants adequate notice of the claims against them
and the grounds upon which each claim rests.' Weiland, 792 F.3d at 1323. The Court
is therefore required to direct Plaintiffs to replead their complaint."

--- [Doc. 35 at 7] (pro se leave to amend) ---
"The Court also acknowledges that because Plaintiffs are proceeding pro se, the
Court must give Plaintiffs an opportunity to amend their complaint in order before
dismissing the complaint with prejudice. See Lee v. Alachua Cnty., 461 F. App'x 859,
860 (11th Cir. Feb. 21, 2012) (explaining that where a 'more carefully drafted
complaint might state a claim,' the court must allow a pro se plaintiff 'at least
one chance to amend the complaint before the district court dismisses the action
with prejudice,' unless amendment would be futile)."

--- [Doc. 35 at 8] (OPERATIVE DIRECTIVE — repleading requirements) ---
"Plaintiffs are therefore DIRECTED to file an amended complaint, NO LATER THAN
AUGUST 21, 2026, in which they correct the deficiencies described above in their
shotgun Complaint by filing an amended complaint that complies with the requirements
of Rule 8(a) and 10(b) and the pleading standards of Twombly and Iqbal by setting
out each of their factual allegations in separate numbered paragraphs; setting out
each of her claims against each Defendant, each of which they must properly
identify, in numbered Counts, making clear which claims are asserted against which
Defendant; identifying within each claim the statute, contractual right, or tort
that forms the basis for each claim; and for each claim, providing the facts
relevant to each Defendant that support that claim, without incorporating by
reference all preceding paragraphs and/or Counts.

Plaintiffs should consider Defendants' assertions in their motions to dismiss
concerning the deficiencies in the Complaint and correct those deficiencies in their
Amended Complaint as they might not be given another opportunity to amend their
pleadings. Plaintiffs' amended complaint will supersede their original Complaint, so
they must include all of their allegations and claims in the amended complaint.

Plaintiffs are cautioned that if they do not comply with this Order, the undersigned
may recommend to the District Judge that this case be dismissed."
