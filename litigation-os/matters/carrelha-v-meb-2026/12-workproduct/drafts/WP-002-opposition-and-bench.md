## What this is, and what it is not

This analyses two things the record usually leaves to assumption: who is on the
other side, and who is on the bench. Both were checked against primary sources
this session — the CourtListener RECAP docket system and the full text of the
opinions themselves — rather than taken from the intelligence documents in the
client's own repository.

That distinction turned out to matter. The repository contains six substantial
Keith Anderson documents. Verifying them produced one strong confirmation and
five defects, two of which would be serious if they reached a filing.

Labels: `[VERIFIED]` a primary source was read; `[ALLEGED]` a party or a
document asserts it; `[COURT-FOUND]` a court found it; `[INFERENCE]` drawn from
verified facts; `[UNRESOLVED]` the record does not answer it.

No commercial citator was available, so no authority below is described as good
law above **citator-limited medium** confidence. No PACER access was available,
so no docket entry in this case has been read.

## Part I — Opposing counsel

### What was verified

`[VERIFIED]` **Caudell v. NewRez LLC, No. 3:26-cv-00007 (M.D. Ga.), Doc. 17
(Apr. 28, 2026).** The docket entry reads, verbatim: "ORDER granting in part
and denying in part NewRez LLC d/b/a Shellpoint Mortgage Service and Federal
National Mortgage Association's [12] Motion to Dismiss [10] Amended Complaint
for failure to state a claim and lifting [14] stay of discovery imposed on
2/27/2026. Ordered by US DISTRICT JUDGE TILMAN E. SELF, III on 4/28/2026."

Three things follow, and one does not.

It follows that a NewRez/Shellpoint motion to dismiss an amended complaint was
granted only **in part**. It follows that a discovery stay had been imposed and
was lifted at the same moment the motion was resolved. And it follows that
*Caudell* reached that point through the same sequence this matter is in — an
earlier motion resolved at Doc. 11, a repleading, then a second motion.

What does **not** follow is which counts survived. The docket text does not say.
The claim in the client's repository that RESPA and breach of contract survived
is `[UNRESOLVED]`, and should not be asserted until Doc. 17 itself is read.

`[VERIFIED]` Sixty-nine N.D. Ga. dockets carry a "Keith Anderson" attorney
appearance. Two of the cases named in the repository check out on the exact
docket numbers given: *Harris v. NewRez LLC*, 1:24-cv-03950 (Grimberg, J., filed
Sept. 4, 2024) and *Williams v. Bank of New York Mellon*, 1:24-cv-04624 (May, J.,
filed Oct. 11, 2024). *Carley v. Seterus, Inc.*, 1:16-cv-02184 (Cooper, J.),
also checks out.

`[UNRESOLVED]` **His appearance in this case is not confirmed by any court
record.** The RECAP docket here carries no entries, so it carries no attorney.
A name match in an attorney index is not an identification, and sixty-nine
dockets spanning 2002 to 2026 are not necessarily one person.

### What failed verification

**The citation strategy is built on two readings that the opinions do not
support.** These are the findings that matter most.

`[VERIFIED]` **Ames does not reserve a void-assignment exception.** The
repository instructs pleading the assignment void rather than voidable and
citing "Ames v. JP Morgan 298 Ga. at 741 (reservation)." Every occurrence of
"void" in *Ames v. JP Morgan Chase Bank, N.A.*, 298 Ga. 732 (2016), is in
footnote 8, and footnote 8 is a survey of other jurisdictions. Its text places
Georgia on the stricter side:

> Although it appears that all courts have held that plaintiff debtors lack
> standing to challenge a *voidable* assignment of the security instrument,
> some courts have indicated—consistent with our conclusion—that plaintiff
> debtors *always* lack standing to challenge an assignment when they have
> failed to prove an injury from the assignment … while others have held or
> suggested that there is debtor standing to challenge an assignment that is
> truly *void*.

The void line is described as what *other* courts do, expressly contrasted with
"our conclusion." *Ames* is the controlling Georgia Supreme Court authority and
it runs against this argument, not for it.

`[VERIFIED]` **Racette says nothing about assignments.** The repository cites
*Racette v. Bank of America, N.A.*, 318 Ga. App. 171 (2012) as "direct authority
for borrower attack on void assignments." A full-text search of the opinion
returns **zero** occurrences of the word "assignment." *Racette* is about
published foreclosure advertisements that misdescribed a senior lien by deed
book and page.

Citing a real case for a holding it does not contain is the fact pattern behind
most Rule 11 citation inquiries. If either citation reached a filed pleading, it
needs correcting rather than defending.

`[VERIFIED]` **A fact was imported from the template case.** The QuickRef
instructs pleading that the chain "terminates before reaching Fannie Mae REMIC
Trust 2024-91." Fannie Mae is a co-defendant in *Caudell*. No source in this
matter names Fannie Mae or any REMIC Trust 2024-91; the asserted owners here are
MEB Loan Trust VIII and FREED Mortgage Trust 2022-HE1. Pleading a trust with no
connection to this loan would be a gift to the defence that nobody on the other
side would even have to find.

`[VERIFIED]` **The repository contradicts itself on two verifiable facts.** The
May 3 documents give the case number as 2:26-cv-00110-**SCJ**-AWH, implying
Judge Steve C. Jones; the docket records Story and Howard. The same documents
describe Bradley Arant as "fka McCalla Raymer Leibert Pierce" and say Anderson
previously practised there; the August document expressly retracts this. Bradley
Arant and McCalla Raymer are separate firms, and McCalla Raymer is separately a
party-adjacent entity in this matter.

### What was recovered

`[VERIFIED]` *Racette* is genuinely useful — for a theory nobody was pursuing.
It reversed dismissal of wrongful-foreclosure, breach-of-contract and
emotional-distress claims where the published advertisements referenced a senior
lien at a deed book and page concerning a **different property and different
borrowers**, and where the borrower gave actual notice of the error on the
courthouse steps. The court held it could not decide at the pleading stage
whether a bidder could have resolved the uncertainty from county records.

`[INFERENCE]` That makes the published notices of foreclosure sale — recorded
here as a priority-3 gap — worth pulling now. If they contain a comparable
defect, this matter has a claim with Georgia appellate authority holding it
survives a motion to dismiss, carrying emotional-distress damages, and entirely
independent of who owns the note.

`[VERIFIED]` **The § 44-14-162(b) theory faces authority *Ames* itself cites.**
*Ames* describes *Haynes v. McCalla Raymer, LLC*, 793 F.3d 1246, 1252–53 (11th
Cir. 2015), as "holding that debtors lacked standing to challenge an assignment
using OCGA § 44-14-162(b) on the ground that the assignment was not properly
attested." *Haynes* has not been read and must be, before that count is pleaded
or abandoned. *Ames* also cites *Duke Galish, LLC v. Southcrest Bank*, 314 Ga.
App. 801, 803 (2012), "leaving open the question of whether a failure to comply
with OCGA § 44-14-162(b) rendered the sale void or voidable" — but that open
question is about the **sale**, not about standing to attack an assignment, and
the two must not be run together.

### What to expect procedurally

`[INFERENCE]` A motion to dismiss and a motion to stay discovery, filed
together. The stay is the one verified element of the pattern: *Caudell* shows a
stay imposed and later lifted. An opposition to a discovery stay should be
drafted before it is needed, not after.

## Part II — The bench

### Judge Richard W. Story

`[VERIFIED]` Presiding district judge; Magistrate Judge Anna W. Howard is the
referred magistrate. This also settles the caption: RWS-AWH, not SCJ-AWH.

`[VERIFIED]` At least 117 RECAP dockets in this district match wrongful
foreclosure and security deed terms, running from 2010 to the present. RECAP
holds only what somebody purchased, so that is a floor rather than a census.

`[INFERENCE]` The practical consequence is about brief construction, not
outcome. A judge who has seen this doctrine for fifteen years does not need it
restated. Length spent on settled Georgia foreclosure law is length not spent on
what distinguishes this record — a principal balance frozen for two years while
fees accrue, a tender that was never reversed, two records naming different
owners.

`[INFERENCE]` In the twenty most relevant dockets read, every one carries a
termination date, most between roughly four and eighteen months from filing.
That is a ranked sample, not a random one, and termination says nothing about
who won. It is stated because the shape matters for sequencing. **No percentage
should appear in any filing on this basis.**

`[COURT-FOUND]` Doc. 34 (SRC-010). In this matter the court denied remand, denied the preliminary
injunction and oral-hearing motions as moot, and ordered that Defendants give
Plaintiffs and the Court at least sixty days' advance notice before any
foreclosure. `[INFERENCE]` That condition was imposed in terms nobody requested.
A court that imposes a protective condition of its own motion is not treating
the foreclosure risk as imaginary.

### Magistrate Judge Anna W. Howard

`[VERIFIED]` The standing referral magistrate for matters assigned to Judge
Story in the Gainesville Division, with at least 699 N.D. Ga. referrals. Every
2:26-cv docket assigned to Story in the returned set is referred to her. The
referral here is routine, not a signal.

`[VERIFIED]` **No published opinion authored by her could be located.** Searches
on her name and on her surname within this district returned nothing. Anyone
claiming to predict her tendencies from case law is not working from the record.
That is not the same as having no written record: reports and recommendations
are frequently unpublished and live only on the docket, which is another reason
the docket matters.

`[INFERENCE]` It follows that **Doc. 35 is the single most important source on
how she reasons, and it is in hand.** It should be read as a specification to be
satisfied clause by clause, not as background.

`[COURT-FOUND]` Doc. 35 (SRC-011) held the complaint to be a type-one shotgun pleading,
directed an amended complaint by August 21, 2026, denied Docs. 4 and 5 as moot
with leave to renew, gave Defendants fourteen days from filing to respond, and
cautioned that dismissal could follow.

`[VERIFIED]` Set beside *Vibe Micro, Inc. v. Shabanets*, 878 F.3d 1291, 1309
(11th Cir. 2018) — where the court declined to give a further chance to replead
because the district court had "provided him with specific instructions on how
to properly do so" and "[h]e did not fix it" — compliance with the terms of
Doc. 35 is the dispositive procedural fact in this matter. Not the merits of any
count. Compliance.

`[VERIFIED]` The same opinion holds at 1308–09 that state-law claims dismissed
on Rule 8 grounds should be dismissed **without prejudice as to refiling in
state court**, which preserves the Georgia claims if the federal case does not
survive. And at 1308 n.6 it expressly reserves the position of a party
proceeding pro se: it decides the question neither way, and should not be cited
as though it did.

`[INFERENCE]` Her referral docket carries a heavy volume of consumer-reporting
matters. A pleading organised the way an FCRA or RESPA complaint is organised —
each communication categorised, each statutory subsection identified, each item
of damage particularised — is in a form she reads constantly.

## What this analysis cannot tell you

No filing in this case has been read. Whether an amended complaint was filed,
what it says, whether it carries the *Ames* and *Racette* citations, whether a
motion to dismiss followed, and who signed it, are all unknown, and all knowable
only from the docket. Every conclusion above is bounded by that.
