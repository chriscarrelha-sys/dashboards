The three findings that carry the most weight

1 · The ledger stops moving, and the fee column does not

[VERIFIED] The servicing history records a principal balance frozen at $140,288.02 from 21 December 2023 onward, while twenty-seven fee entries totalling $8,408.74 continue to accrue against the account.

[VERIFIED] Four 2025 trial-plan tenders of $1,899.56 each appear. Three are reversed by entries booked at $0.00. The fourth, tendered 17 May 2025, is never reversed, and its amount equals the ending unapplied balance.

[INFERENCE] The unreversed tender and the ending unapplied balance being the same figure is consistent with a payment received and never applied. It is not proof of one. The servicer's transaction-code key and cash-application detail would settle it, and neither has been produced.

2 · Two records name different owners

[VERIFIED] A MERS ServicerID result retrieved 24 March 2026 names FREED Mortgage Trust 2022-HE1 as investor. [ALLEGED] A Shellpoint letter names MEB Loan Trust VIII Trust, c/o US Bank Trust National Association. [ALLEGED] The assignment pleaded at Deed Book 11187, page 717, recites MEB as a corporation organised under the laws of New York.

[UNRESOLVED] No note, allonge, security deed, assignment instrument, trust agreement or loan schedule has been read. The conflict is recorded; its significance is not established, and a borrower's standing to raise it is an open legal question.

3 · The funding gap is the count that does not depend on standing

[ALLEGED] The origination is pleaded as a 21 July 2022 line of credit from Lendage LLC with a $149,650.00 limit, of which $51,409.50 is alleged to have reached the borrower's account on 5 August 2022.

[INFERENCE] A failure-of-consideration theory turns on what was funded rather than on who owns the instrument, so it does not depend on resolving the ownership conflict. [UNRESOLVED] The bank records that would establish what was actually funded have not been produced.

The risk that should be stated plainly

[VERIFIED] In Vibe Micro, Inc. v. Shabanets, 878 F.3d 1291, 1309 (11th Cir. 2018), the court wrote that the plaintiff "is not entitled to another chance to replead," having been given "specific instructions on how to properly do so," and declined to "adopt a rule requiring district courts to endure endless shotgun pleadings."

[INFERENCE] Doc. 35 gave specific repleading instructions. An amended complaint that does not follow them faces this authority directly. [VERIFIED] The same opinion holds at 1308–09 that state-law claims dismissed on Rule 8 grounds should be dismissed without prejudice as to refiling in state court, which preserves the Georgia claims if the federal case does not survive.

[UNRESOLVED] Vibe Micro expressly reserved the position of a party proceeding pro se, at 1308 n.6. It does not decide the question either way, and should not be cited as though it did.
