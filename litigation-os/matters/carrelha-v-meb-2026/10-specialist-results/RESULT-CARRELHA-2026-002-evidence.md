## 1. Assignment answered

The objective was to establish what the available record proves about initial
loan funding, ownership and transfer, and the payment-application allegations.
**Answered in part.**

- **Payment application: answered.** The servicer's complete 5-page loan history
  was read line by line and is analyzed below.
- **Funding: answered in the negative.** The record contains no evidence of
  delivery of loan proceeds in either direction. That is a finding, not a failure
  to look.
- **Ownership and transfer: answered only weakly.** The single source is a
  background footnote in an order deciding a remand motion. The recorded
  instruments themselves have not been examined and are publicly obtainable.

## 2. Short conclusion

The defendants' own ledger does not contain the $5,200.00 wire on which the
pleading's breach, RESPA, and FDCPA theories rest. It does contain a documented
payment the pleading does not use: $1,899.56 tendered on 2025-05-17, never
reversed, still sitting unapplied in suspense ten months later while foreclosure
fees accrued. On funding and on the validity of the assignment, the available
record is silent.

## 3. Verified findings

### A. What the servicer's ledger establishes

1. `[VERIFIED]` Principal has been frozen at **$140,288.02** since 2023-12-21 and
   had not moved as of the 2026-03-10 print. — SRC-013, p.3 row 12/21/23; totals
   block p.5.
2. `[VERIFIED]` From 2024-05-06 through 2026-02-27 the account was charged a
   continuous series of default-related disbursements — property inspection,
   title, attorney, foreclosure costs, certified mail, BPO/appraisal — every one
   carrying a Due Date of 01/01/24. — SRC-013, pp.1–3.
3. `[VERIFIED]` **Four web payments of $1,899.56 each** were tendered between
   2025-05-02 and 2025-06-18. Three (05/02, 05/10, 06/18) were reversed under Rev
   Code 2, each paired with an NSF Fee Assessment showing a **$0.00** amount. The
   fourth, **2025-05-17, was never reversed**, and the totals block shows an
   "Ending Unapplied Balance $1,899.56" matching it. — SRC-013, p.2; totals p.5.
   **This is the strongest documented payment-application fact in the matter, and
   it comes from the defendants' own record.**
4. `[VERIFIED]` A single suspense credit of **$19,588.88** was received
   2023-09-13 and distributed the next day across due dates from 02/2023 through
   10/2023 by roughly eighteen offsetting entries booked on one day. — SRC-013,
   pp.3–4.
5. `[VERIFIED]` Payments posted on 2022-12-01 and on 2023-03-14 were reversed
   under Rev Code 7 within days, and in the March 2023 instance a late charge
   waived on 03/14 was re-assessed on 03/23. The ledger states no reason. —
   SRC-013, pp.4–5.
6. `[VERIFIED]` The opening row (2022-08-05) shows a **Late Charge Balance of
   $1,145.62 already present on the origination-date row**. — SRC-013, p.5.
   What that shows is what the ledger displays; it does not itself show that a
   late charge was wrongly assessed at origination, and the balance may be an
   artifact of how the prior servicer's data was converted.
7. `[VERIFIED]` Shellpoint booked the loan on 2024-07-07 ("New Loan", $0.00,
   opening principal $140,288.02, late-charge balance $624.00). — SRC-013, p.3.

### E. A defect in the pleading itself

12. `[VERIFIED]` Each of the nine Counts of the First Amended Complaint
    realleges "Paragraphs 1 through 46," but the pleading's numbering restarts at
    1 in each Roman-numeral section, so at least nine paragraphs bear the number
    1 and none is uniquely 46. Counting continuously, the sections run 2 + 4 + 7
    + 6 + 5 + 7 + 3 + 8 + 4 = **exactly 46**, ending at the last factual
    allegation — so the drafter's intent is unambiguous and the rendering is the
    only problem. — SRC-001. See CON-007.
13. `[VERIFIED]` The First Amended Complaint's signature and verification blocks
    read "this Date day of August, 2026" and "By: Person" — unfilled template
    placeholders. As held in this manifest it is an unexecuted draft. — SRC-001.
14. `[VERIFIED]` The First Amended Complaint names Lendage, LLC in its caption
    but asserts **no count against it**. — SRC-001. Doc. 34 at 21 had held Lendage
    was not a defendant.

### F. A correction to existing matter work product

15. `[VERIFIED]` The annotated spreadsheet SRC-007 labels the 2022-08-05 row
    "RED FLAG: NEGATIVE principal payment: $-149,650.00." Read against the
    original ledger, that row is the **opening-balance booking** — it establishes
    the $149,650.00 principal balance. Characterizing it as an anomalous negative
    payment is wrong, and advancing it would be a serious credibility error.
    The 2022-11-16 (-$2,900.00) and 2023-02-09 (-$1,700.00) rows are different:
    they increase the balance with no matching prior credit, and on a HELOC the
    most likely innocent explanation is an additional draw. Cause `[UNRESOLVED]`
    without the servicer's transaction-code key. — SRC-007 vs SRC-013; CON-003.

## 5. Contrary information

Stated against our own side, deliberately:

- **The ledger supports the defendants on the 2025 payments.** Three of the four
  web payments were reversed with NSF events. A servicer that receives a returned
  payment ordinarily does not apply it. Only the fourth payment helps us.
- **The court's own background recitation contradicts the pleaded modification.**
  "Loss mitigation efforts … have been unsuccessful" (Doc. 34 at 3 n.4).
- **The $5,200.00 wire is not merely unsupported; the best document affirmatively
  omits it.** That is a materially worse position than an unproven allegation.
- **The FCRA figures cut against themselves in part.** The pleaded October 2025
  balance of $173,839.00 does not match the servicer's principal of $140,288.02 —
  but the difference is roughly consistent with accrued interest and fees, so a
  factfinder could find the reported figure explicable rather than impossible.
- **Two of the seven register entries are not genuine conflicts** (CON-002,
  CON-004), and are recorded as such so they are not argued.

**Where I looked and found nothing contrary:** every row of SRC-013 was read;
SRC-010 and SRC-011 were read in full including footnotes; SRC-012 was read in
full including the notices pages. I found no document supporting the $5,200.00
wire, the executed modification, or the Mercer Advisors rescission — but this
manifest holds none of the client's own files, so that absence is weak evidence
about the world and strong evidence about what is currently provable.
