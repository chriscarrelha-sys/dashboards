## 1. Assignment answered

The objective was to determine whether the draft First Amended Complaint's
repleading method satisfies Doc. 35 and Eleventh Circuit shotgun-pleading law.
**Answered in full.** The controlling authority is directly on point and was read
rather than recalled.

Research questions as framed:

1. Under Eleventh Circuit law, is a complaint a type-one shotgun pleading where
   each count realleges a block of general factual allegations but does **not**
   incorporate the preceding counts?
2. What consequence follows a second deficient pleading after a court-ordered
   repleading, and does that consequence differ for a **pro se** plaintiff?
3. What adverse authority defines the boundary of any safe harbour in (1)?

## 2. Short conclusion

On question 1: no. *Weiland* holds in terms that re-alleging a block of factual
paragraphs at the head of each count "looks, at first glance, like the most
common type of shotgun pleading. **But it is not.**" The draft's structure is the
approved one.

On question 2: a second failure is a real dismissal risk, but *Vibe Micro*'s
holding is stated for **counselled** litigants and its footnote 6 expressly
reserves the pro se question. Doc. 35's own authority for the pro se rule is an
unpublished disposition.

On question 3: the boundary is *Magluta* — a factual block **plus** preceding
counts is fatal. The draft does not do that.

## 3. Verified findings

**F-1. The four categories, and which one Doc. 35 applied.** `[LEGAL-CONCLUSION]`
*Weiland v. Palm Beach Cnty. Sheriff's Office*, 792 F.3d 1313, 1321–23 (11th Cir.
2015), sets out four categories. Type one is "a complaint containing multiple
counts where each count adopts the allegations of all preceding counts." Doc. 35
placed the original complaint in type one, citing ¶¶ 93, 103, 111, 122, 127, 133
and 138 — the paragraphs by which each of the seven original counts incorporated
its predecessors. SRC-011, Doc. 35 at 7.

**F-2. The draft is not in that category.** `[LEGAL-CONCLUSION]` *Weiland*, 792
F.3d at 1324:

> "Weiland's re-alleging of paragraphs 1 through 49 at the beginning of each
> count looks, at first glance, like the most common type of shotgun pleading.
> **But it is not.**"

The Eleventh Circuit reversed the dismissal of those counts. The parallel to our
draft is close to exact: *Weiland*'s counts re-alleged "paragraphs 1 through 49";
the draft's Counts reallege "Paragraphs 1 through 46." Neither incorporates a
preceding count. **This is the answer to the assignment.**

**F-3. Where the boundary lies.** `[LEGAL-CONCLUSION]` In the same passage
*Weiland* distinguishes *Magluta v. Samples*, 256 F.3d 1282, 1284 (11th Cir.
2001), where each count incorporated a 146-paragraph "General Factual
Allegations" section "**while also incorporating** the allegations of a[ll
preceding counts]." The conjunction is the whole point: a factual block alone is
permissible; a factual block *plus* preceding counts is not. I did not open
*Magluta* itself — the quotation above is *Weiland*'s parenthetical, and the row
is marked accordingly.

**F-4. The standard is demanding.** `[LEGAL-CONCLUSION]` *Weiland*, 792 F.3d at
1325–26: dismissal under Rules 8(a)(2) and 10(b) is appropriate where "it is
*virtually impossible* to know which allegations of fact are intended to support
which claim(s) for relief," and the court found "[n]o such virtual impossibility"
there. The draft here identifies for each Count the statute or doctrine, the
defendants it runs against, and the facts relied on — well clear of virtual
impossibility.

**F-5. The consequence of a second failure — with a material limit.**
`[LEGAL-CONCLUSION]` *Vibe Micro, Inc. v. Shabanets*, 878 F.3d 1291, 1308 (11th
Cir. 2018):

> "When a litigant files a shotgun pleading, **is represented by counsel**, and
> fails to request leave to amend, a district court must sua sponte give him one
> chance to replead before dismissing his case with prejudice on non-merits
> shotgun pleading grounds."

And at 1308 n.6:

> "Here, as in *Daewoo*, we 'decide and intimate nothing about a party proceeding
> pro se.'"

Two distinctions from Mandel both favour Plaintiffs: he was counselled throughout
and **conceded on appeal** that his second pleading remained a shotgun pleading.
Neither is true here on the draft reviewed.

**F-6. A downside limit worth knowing.** `[LEGAL-CONCLUSION]` *Vibe Micro*, 878
F.3d at 1308–09: where federal claims are dismissed on non-merits Rule 8 grounds,
supplemental state-law claims "should have been without prejudice as to refiling
in state court." This matter carries Georgia claims — wrongful foreclosure,
slander of title, breach of contract, the implied covenant, and the GFBPA.
Even a worst-case Rule 8 dismissal should not extinguish them. Found while
searching for adverse authority, and recorded because the assignment was framed
on risk.

**F-7. The authority behind Doc. 35's pro se rule is unpublished.**
`[INFERENCE]` Doc. 35 at 7 relies on *Lee v. Alachua Cnty.*, 461 F. App'x 859,
860 (11th Cir. Feb. 21, 2012). Two CourtListener searches returned zero results
and I could not open it. The "F. App'x" designation is a strong indicator of a
non-precedential disposition, but **that inference is not confirmed** against the
court's own rule. Premises: (a) Doc. 35 cites it in that reporter; (b) that
reporter carries unpublished dispositions. This dovetails with *Vibe Micro* n.6:
the pro se rule in this circuit rests on unpublished authority plus an express
reservation, so neither side should treat it as settled.

**F-8. Remaining defects — different problems, not the Doc. 35 problem.**
`[LEGAL-CONCLUSION]`

- *The cross-reference is ambiguous on its face.* The draft's numbering restarts
  in each Roman-numeral section, so no paragraph is uniquely "46." Counting
  continuously the sections run 2 + 4 + 7 + 6 + 5 + 7 + 3 + 8 + 4 = **exactly
  46**, so the intent is unambiguous and only the rendering is wrong. Doc. 35
  required each factual allegation "in separate numbered paragraphs"; a continuous
  renumbering satisfies that and removes the issue entirely. Cheap to fix, and
  needlessly risky to leave.
- *Lendage, LLC is in the caption with no count against it.* Doc. 34 at 21 held
  Lendage "was not, and is not, a named defendant," and Doc. 34 at 22 n.11
  observed that Plaintiffs' grievances "are not directed at Lendage, LLC." A
  caption-only defendant is the fourth *Weiland* category in miniature — a
  defendant who cannot tell which claim is brought against it because none is.
- *The draft as held is unexecuted*, with "this Date day of August, 2026" and
  "By: Person" placeholders in the signature and verification blocks (SRC-001).

## 5. Contrary information

Searched for deliberately, by running the opponent's argument rather than ours:

1. **The strongest contrary authority is *Magluta*, and it is cited inside our
   best case.** If the amended pleading incorporated *both* the factual block
   *and* preceding counts, *Magluta* would control and the pleading would fail.
   The draft does not, but this is the line to stay behind.
2. ***Vibe Micro* is squarely adverse on consequence.** A district court may
   dismiss with prejudice after one chance, and the Eleventh Circuit will not
   "adopt a rule requiring district courts to endure endless shotgun pleadings"
   (878 F.3d at 1309). Footnote 6 limits it to counselled parties; it does not
   erase the risk.
3. **A *Weiland* safe harbour on type one does not immunise the other three
   categories.** A pleading can escape type one and still be dismissed as
   conclusory and vague (type two), for not separating claims (type three), or —
   most relevant here, because of Lendage — for naming a defendant without
   specifying the claim against it (type four).
4. **Doc. 35 required more than not-incorporating-counts.** It directed Plaintiffs
   to identify for each claim the statute, contractual right, or tort, and the
   facts relevant to *each Defendant*. A pleading can satisfy *Weiland* and still
   fall short of this particular order.

**Where I searched and found nothing further contrary:** CourtListener's Eleventh
Circuit opinion database, by case name and by full-text literal search within
*Weiland* and *Vibe Micro* for language qualifying either holding. I did **not**
search a commercial database, and I could not run a citator. I located no
Eleventh Circuit decision narrowing *Weiland*'s factual-block holding — but the
absence of such a decision in the searches I ran is not the same as its
non-existence.
